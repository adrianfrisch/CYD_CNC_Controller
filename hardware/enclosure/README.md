# Enclosures for the supported display boards

Parametric, two-part, 3D-printable enclosures for the three boards the firmware supports. Each one has a cable channel and a screw-down clamp, so the CAT5 cable to the GRBL Arduino can't pull on the small JST connector.

| Board (PlatformIO env) | Display | Enclosure (without / with cable tongue) | Cable route |
|---|---|---|---|
| [`esp32-8048S070C`](#esp32-8048s070c-7) | 7" 800×480, capacitive | 185.6 × 112.6 × 18.3 / 199.1 × 128.6 mm | P1 on the left wall → groove along the wall **up** → clamp past the top-left corner |
| [`esp32-4827S043R`](#esp32-4827s043r-43) | 4.3" 480×272, resistive | 127.6 × 78.6 × 17.3 / 141.1 × 94.6 mm | P1 on the left wall → groove along the wall **down** (P1 is below USB-C here) → clamp past the bottom-left corner |
| [`esp32-2432S028R`](#esp32-2432s028r-28-cyd) | 2.8" 320×240 CYD, resistive | 90.6 × 54.6 × 16.4 / 90.6 × 76.6 mm | P3/CN1 on the bottom edge → pocket → clamp straight below |

> **Read this before printing.** Several dimensions are **estimated**, because no primary drawing gives them (see each board's dimension table). Measure the items in the board's *Measure before printing* list with calipers. Put your values into `boards/<board>.py` and rebuild. The fit checks only show that the CAD agrees with the model of the board in the script. They don't show that the model matches your physical board. The 7" design has been test-printed and fits; the 4.3" and 2.8" designs have not been printed yet.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|------|------------|
| `enclosure.py` | Shared parametric CadQuery builder: design defaults, geometry, checks, export |
| `boards/<board>.py` | Board data: every dimension as a named constant with its source or "EST", plus board-specific settings (vents, keyholes, cable route) |
| `<env>/bezel.step` | Front bezel, in assembly coordinates |
| `<env>/back_shell.step` | Back shell (with the cable channel and clamp tongue), in assembly coordinates |
| `<env>/clamp.step` | Cable clamp bar, in assembly coordinates (screwed down on the cable) |
| `<env>/assembly.step` | Bezel + shell + clamp + a simple board placeholder (PCB, LCD, glass, connectors, switches, ESP32 module, SMD envelope) for fit checking |
| `<env>/bezel.stl`, `back_shell.stl`, `clamp.stl` | Parts in print orientation |
| `<env>/preview_*.png` | *Not committed — generated by `enclosure.py`:* hidden-line views and colour cross-sections through every opening, the cable channel and the clamp |
| `reference/` | *Not committed (third-party copyright)* — download the vendor drawings, datasheets, schematics, photos and models from the sources listed per board |

### Rebuild

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install cadquery cairosvg matplotlib
.venv/bin/python enclosure.py                      # all boards
.venv/bin/python enclosure.py esp32-2432S028R      # one board
.venv/bin/python enclosure.py --no-png             # skip the previews
```

The script exits non-zero if a check fails. For each board it checks that:

- the exported STEP files re-import cleanly;
- the board placeholder doesn't intersect either shell or the clamp, and the shells don't intersect each other;
- the mating bodies (USB plug + overmould, JST plug, micro-SD card, 3 mm pin to each button) pass through their cut-outs untouched;
- a CAT5 cable of `CABLE_D` passes the whole channel untouched, and the clamp actually squeezes it;
- the bezel window doesn't cover the LCD active area, whichever way round the active-area offsets go.

Current result: **all checks pass for all three boards**. Every interference volume is 0.0000 mm³.

## Design (all boards)

**Coordinates (as seen from the front):** X runs from the left PCB edge, Y from the bottom edge. Z = 0 is the PCB front face, and +Z points towards you.

- **Front bezel**
  - 2 mm front plate. The window overlaps the glass / touch panel edge by 2.0 mm on every side and stays clear of the active area.
  - 0.2 mm of air above the glass, so the bezel never presses on the touch panel (important for the resistive panels: pressure there would register as touches).
  - Four Ø7 corner bosses rest on the PCB front face next to the LCD. Each takes an M3 heat-set insert.
  - A rabbet around the inside of the walls receives the shell's alignment lip.
- **Back shell**
  - 2 mm floor and walls, 0.3 mm clearance around the PCB outline, 7.0 mm under the PCB back face.
  - Four Ø7 standoffs at the PCB mounting holes, with counterbored M3 clearance holes from outside.
  - Cut-outs for USB, the GRBL cable connector, the micro-SD slot (with a finger scallop), Ø4 RESET/BOOT access holes (Ø3.4 on the CYD), ventilation slots.
  - Two **keyholes** for wall or machine mounting (Ø8 head entry, 4 mm slot). They fit screws with heads up to about 7.5 mm across and 2.5 mm high.
- **Fastening.** Four M3 screws go in from the back. Each passes through a shell standoff, the PCB mounting hole, and into the heat-set insert in the bezel boss. The PCB ends up clamped between the standoffs and the bezel bosses, and the same screws hold the two halves together.
- **Optional:** other edge connectors are modelled as placeholders in `PORTS` with `cut=False`; set `cut=True` to open them in the wall.

### Cable channel and clamp

A standard CAT5/5e cable runs to the GRBL Arduino. It must not pull on the small JST connector.

- **Channel** (`route="side"`, 7" and 4.3"): a U-groove (6.5 mm wide, for a 6.0 mm jacket) moulded onto the outside of the left wall. It starts at the P1 opening and runs along the side, away from the USB-C port, to the corner. It is open towards the front: press the cable in past the snap lips before mounting the case. Its floor is flush with the case back, so the keyhole mounting still sits flat. The groove continues onto a 16 mm tongue past the corner.
- **Pocket** (`route="straight"`, CYD): the connectors face the bottom wall, so the cable goes straight down. A pocket as wide as the opening (27 mm) collects the pigtail from either connector and leads into the groove on a 22 mm tongue below the case.
- **Clamp:** the separate **clamp bar** (`clamp.stl`) screws down onto the tongue with two M3 × 8 screws into heat-set inserts. A ridge with grip teeth reaches into the groove and squeezes the jacket by 0.8 mm (`CLAMP_SQUEEZE`), so all pull goes into the case, not the connector. The groove floor is rounded where the cable leaves the clamp, so it can't kink on a sharp edge.
- **Wiring:** CAT5 conductors (24 AWG solid) don't fit 1.25 mm JST crimp contacts. Strip the CAT5 jacket just behind the opening and solder the conductors to a short JST 1.25 4-pin pigtail; insulate the splices with heat-shrink and lay them in the groove. Leave a little slack between the clamp and the plug. Wire assignment (signal + GND per pair): see the wiring section in the main README.
- **Route:** set in the board file, e.g. `CABLE = dict(port="p1_uart", route="side", run="top")`. A route that crosses the USB port fails the plug-overmould check.

### Hardware (per enclosure)

| Qty | Part |
|-----|------|
| 4 | M3 heat-set insert, short type (M3 × 4 mm, OD ≈ 4.6 mm). The hole is Ø4.0 × 6 mm deep; adjust `INSERT_HOLE_D` for your inserts. |
| 4 | M3 × 12 socket-head (ISO 4762) or button-head (ISO 7380) screw. The script prints the required length. |
| 2 | M3 heat-set insert (same type) for the cable clamp tongue |
| 2 | M3 × 8 socket- or button-head screw for the cable clamp |
| 2 | Wall screws for the keyholes (shank ≤ 3.8 mm, head 5–7.5 mm) |
| 1 | JST 1.25 mm (Molex PicoBlade-compatible) 4-pin pigtail, spliced to the CAT5 cable. One comes with the board. |
| 1 | Standard CAT5/5e cable to the Arduino/GRBL |
| 1 | Small zip tie (≤ 2.5 mm wide), optional, for the pigtail inside (7" and 4.3" have a tie bar next to P1) |

### Print settings (suggestion)

- PETG or ASA is best, because the backlight and the ESP32 get warm. PLA works if the unit stays out of the sun and away from heat.
- 0.2 mm layers, 3 perimeters (about 1.2 mm, so the 2 mm walls are nearly solid), 20–30 % infill.
- **Bezel:** front face down on the bed (that's how the STL is oriented). No supports. Use a smooth or textured PEI sheet for a nice front.
- **Back shell:** back face down. No supports. The wall cut-outs bridge at most 27 mm (CYD cable opening), the others at most 14 mm, the keyhole pockets 8 mm.
- **Cable clamp:** flat top face down (that's how the STL is oriented), no supports.
- **Inserts:** press them into the bezel bosses and the two clamp-tongue holes with a soldering iron at about 220 °C (PETG).
- **Test fit first:** before a full print, print a thin slice of the walls with openings (slicer cut) and check them against your board.

## Known limitations

- None of the vendors publishes a 3D model or a full drawing of the back side. Connector positions come from renders (7" back: about ±1 mm) and, for the CYD, from a community model. Heights come from generic connector datasheets. The cut-outs are generous to cover this.
- The active-area offsets are only documented for the 7" panel. The 4.3" and 2.8" values are typical panel figures; the 2 mm lip leaves 1.0–1.4 mm margin in the worst case.
- The board placeholder in `assembly.step` is a simplified fit-check body. It is not a model of the real board.
- The vendor's "complete package" ZIP for the 7" board on Tinytronics was blocked by Cloudflare; the same package mirrored on GitHub (wegi1) was used instead.
