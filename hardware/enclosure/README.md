# Enclosure for the ESP32-8048S070C (7" CNC touchscreen)

A parametric, two-part, 3D-printable enclosure for the Sunton/Jingcai
**ESP32-8048S070C**: 7" 800×480 RGB TFT, GT911 capacitive touch, ESP32-S3-WROOM-1.

> **Read this before printing.** Several dimensions are **estimated**. No
> primary drawing gives them (see the dimension table). Measure the items in
> [Measure before printing](#measure-before-printing) with calipers. Put your
> values into `enclosure.py` and rebuild. The fit checks only show that the
> CAD agrees with the model of the board in the script. They don't show that
> the model matches your physical board.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|------|------------|
| `enclosure.py` | Single parametric CadQuery script. Every dimension is a named constant at the top, with its source or "EST". |
| `bezel.step` | Front bezel, in assembly coordinates |
| `back_shell.step` | Back shell (with the P1 cable channel and clamp tongue), in assembly coordinates |
| `clamp.step` | Cable clamp bar, in assembly coordinates (screwed down on the cable) |
| `assembly.step` | Bezel + shell + a simple board placeholder (PCB, LCD, glass, connectors, switches, ESP32 module, SMD envelope) for fit checking |
| `bezel.stl` | Bezel in print orientation (front face on the bed) |
| `back_shell.stl` | Back shell in print orientation (back face on the bed) |
| `clamp.stl` | Cable clamp in print orientation (flat top on the bed, ridge up) |
| `preview_*.png` | *Not committed — generated by `enclosure.py`:* hidden-line views (assembly front/back/back-left, bezel inside, shell inside) and colour cross-sections through USB-C, P1, the micro-SD slot, the cable channel and the clamp, plus a view of the channel |
| `reference/` | *Not committed (third-party copyright)* — download the vendor drawings, datasheets, schematics and photos from the sources below |

### Rebuild

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install cadquery cairosvg matplotlib
.venv/bin/python enclosure.py            # build, export, check, render PNGs
.venv/bin/python enclosure.py --no-png   # skip the previews
```

The script exits non-zero if a check fails. It checks four things:

- The exported STEP files re-import cleanly.
- The board placeholder doesn't intersect either shell, and the shells don't intersect each other.
- The mating bodies (USB-C plug overmould, P1 JST plug, micro-SD card, 3 mm pin to each button) pass through their cut-outs untouched.
- The bezel window doesn't cover the LCD active area, whichever way round the LCD drawing is oriented.

Current result: **all checks pass**. Every interference volume is 0.0000 mm³.

## The parts

**Coordinates (as seen from the front):** X runs 0–181 from the left PCB edge. Y runs 0–108 from the bottom edge. Z = 0 is the PCB front face, and +Z points towards you. The USB-C and P1 connectors are on the **left** edge and the micro-SD slot is on the **top** edge. The vendor's pictures of the back are mirrored left to right relative to this frame.

- **Front bezel** (185.6 × 112.6 × 7.7 mm)
  - 2 mm front plate. The window (160.9 × 96.0) overlaps the glass edge by 2.0 mm on every side.
  - Leaves at least 1.9 mm (X) and 2.8 mm (Y) of margin around the 154.08 × 85.92 active area.
  - Leaves 0.2 mm of air above the glass, so the bezel never presses on the touch panel.
  - Four Ø7 corner bosses rest on the PCB front face next to the LCD. Each takes an M3 heat-set insert.
  - A rabbet around the inside of the walls receives the shell's alignment lip.
- **Back shell** (185.6 × 112.6 × 12.1 mm including the 1.5 mm lip)
  - 2 mm floor and walls, with 0.3 mm clearance around the PCB outline and 7.0 mm under the PCB back face.
  - Four Ø7 standoffs at the PCB mounting holes, with counterbored M3 clearance holes from outside.
  - **Cut-outs:**
    - USB-C: 13.0 × 7.5 mm, enough for the plug overmould.
    - P1 UART cable exit: 9 × 5 mm, so a 1.25 mm 4-pin plug passes through. It opens into the cable channel (below). A zip-tie bar on the inside floor next to it can additionally secure the pigtail.
    - Micro-SD slot: 14 × 3 mm, with a finger scallop for the push-push socket.
    - RESET/BOOT access holes: Ø4 in the back, for a pen or pin.
    - Ventilation slots: 2 mm wide, in a large field in the centre and a small field over the ESP32-S3 module.
  - Two **keyholes** for wall or machine mounting: 100 mm apart, Ø8 head entry, 4 mm slot, 10 mm travel. They fit screws with heads up to about 7.5 mm across and 2.5 mm high.
  - An inside relief where the touch-panel FPC wraps around the bottom edge.
  - **P1 cable channel + clamp** for the CAT5 cable to the CNC controller (see below).
- **Assembled size:** 185.6 × 112.6 × 18.3 mm without the channel; 199.1 × 128.6 × 18.3 mm including the channel (8.5 mm beside the left wall) and the clamp tongue (16 mm past the top edge).
- **Optional:** to expose the speaker or extended-IO connectors on the top edge, list them in `EXPOSE_TOP_CONNECTORS`, e.g. `["P6_SPEAKER_JST1.25_2P"]`.

### P1 cable channel and clamp

A standard CAT5/5e cable runs from the P1 connector to the GRBL Arduino. It must not pull on the small JST connector.

- **Channel:** a U-groove (6.5 mm wide, for a 6.0 mm jacket) moulded onto the outside of the left wall. It starts at the P1 opening and runs along the side, away from the USB-C port, to the top-left corner (front view). It is open towards the front: press the cable in past the snap lips before mounting the case. Its floor is flush with the case back, so the keyhole mounting still sits flat.
- **Clamp:** at the corner the groove continues onto a 16 mm tongue past the case edge. The separate **clamp bar** (`clamp.stl`) screws down onto the tongue with two M3 × 8 screws into heat-set inserts. A ridge with grip teeth reaches into the groove and squeezes the jacket by 0.8 mm (`CLAMP_SQUEEZE`), so all pull goes into the case, not the connector. The groove floor is rounded where the cable leaves the clamp, so it can't kink on a sharp edge.
- **Wiring:** CAT5 conductors (24 AWG solid) don't fit 1.25 mm JST crimp contacts. Strip the CAT5 jacket just behind the P1 opening and solder the conductors to a short JST 1.25 4-pin pigtail; insulate the splices with heat-shrink and lay them in the groove. Leave a little slack between the clamp and the plug. Wire assignment (signal + GND per pair): see "7" Board Wiring" in the main README.
- **Direction:** `CHANNEL_DIR = +1` runs the cable towards the top edge. `-1` would run it towards the bottom edge, straight past the USB-C port, which the checks would flag as blocked.

**Fastening.** Four M3 screws go in from the back. Each passes through a shell standoff, then through the PCB mounting hole, then into the heat-set insert in the bezel boss. The PCB ends up clamped between the standoffs and the bezel bosses, and the same screws hold the two halves together.

### Print settings (suggestion)

- PETG or ASA is best, because the backlight and the ESP32-S3 get warm. PLA works if the unit stays out of the sun and away from heat.
- 0.2 mm layers, 3 perimeters (about 1.2 mm, so the 2 mm walls are nearly solid), 20–30 % infill.
- **Bezel:** front face down on the bed (that's how the STL is oriented). Print it without supports. Use a smooth or textured PEI sheet for a nice front.
- **Back shell:** back face down. Print it without supports. The wall cut-outs bridge at most 14 mm, and the keyhole pockets bridge 8 mm.
- **Cable clamp:** flat top face down (that's how the STL is oriented), no supports.
- **Inserts:** press them into the bezel bosses and the two clamp-tongue holes with a soldering iron at about 220 °C (PETG).
- **Test fit first:** before a full print, print a thin slice of the left wall (USB-C + P1) and of the top wall (SD), for example with a slicer cut, and check them against your board.

## Measure before printing

Most important first. All values are in `enclosure.py`.

1. **Front stack: PCB front face to the top of the touch glass** (`LCD_TAPE + LCD_T + GLASS_T`, assumed 5.5 mm). This sets how high the bezel bosses are. If the stack is thicker, the bezel lip presses on the glass. If it's thinner, the glass rattles; add a 0.5 mm foam strip in that case.
2. **Cover-glass outline** (`GLASS_W`, `GLASS_H`, assumed 164.9 × 100 and centred). Check that the glass corner stays ≥ 1 mm clear of the bezel bosses (Ø7 at the hole centres). Also check that the 2 mm lip lands on the glass's black border.
3. **PCB thickness** (`PCB_T`, assumed 1.6). This sets where the clamp and seam sit.
4. **USB-C position and height:** the centre from the top edge (assumed 54.1), the height of the receptacle above the PCB back face (assumed 3.26), and any overhang past the edge.
5. **P1 position** (assumed 38.8 mm from the top edge to the connector centre) and the connector height.
6. **Micro-SD socket:** the mouth's x-range from the left edge (front view: 123.8–134.7) and its height. Also check that a card can be pushed in with a fingernail through the slot.
7. **RESET/BOOT switch positions** (assumed 10.2 and 17.3 mm from the right edge, as seen from the front, and 14.9 mm from the top edge).
8. **Tallest part on the back** (assumed 4.3 mm). If anything is taller than about 4.5 mm near the keyhole pads (x ≈ 33–48 and 133–148, y 66–88), reduce the pads or increase `BACK_CLEAR`.
9. **CAT5 cable jacket diameter** (`CABLE_D`, assumed 6.0 mm; CAT5/5e is typically 5.0–6.5 mm). The groove is `CABLE_D + 0.5` wide and the clamp squeezes 0.8 mm, so set this to your cable's real diameter.
10. **Touch-FPC wrap** at the bottom-right edge (front view): how far it stands out from the PCB edge (relief assumed 1.2 mm).

## Known limitations / problems

- The vendor never published a 3D model or a full drawing of the back side. Connector positions come from a low-resolution render (about ±1 mm) and from the dimensioned front drawing. Heights come from generic connector datasheets. The cut-outs are generous to cover this.
- In the LCD drawing it is unclear which way round the horizontal AA offsets go, i.e. whether the 3.9 mm or the 6.9 mm margin is on the left. The window is sized to work in both cases.
- The board placeholder in `assembly.step` is a simplified fit-check body. It is not a model of the real board.
- The vendor's "complete package" ZIP on Tinytronics was blocked by Cloudflare. I used the same package mirrored on GitHub (wegi1) instead.
